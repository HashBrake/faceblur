# Profile of the production run, 2026-10-16 (T02)

One file, `ego_AZER76400FE_19700101_004100_camera_left_part0001.mp4` (984 frames,
1920x1080 at 30.02 fps), run through `faceblur.batch.run_video` in one process
(`submit=serial_submit`, `workers=1`). The settings are
`Settings(check_output=True, quarantine=True, second_chance=1)`, which is what
`cli.py --quarantine --second-chance 1` builds. The CLI turns `check_output` on
for either flag, and every other flag is at its default (check_stride 1). The
driver is `eval/profile_run.py`:

    py -3.12 "D:/Claude Projects/gpu_lock.py" -- <venv python> eval/profile_run.py <footage>/...004100...mp4 bench_out/profile

The run produced the same result as the T01 baseline record for this file:
11 second-chance seeds, residual faces 11 then 1, 779 masked frames, 60 frames
copied, quarantined, and every model on `DmlExecutionProvider`.

## How it was measured, and what to trust

- **Wall clocks (trusted).** Each phase entry point (`detect_job`, `Tracker.run`,
  `zone.build`/`describe`, `encode_job`, `copy_job`, `segments.concat`/`verify`,
  `verify.plan_jobs`/`summarise`, `verify.residual_job`) and each named part inside
  it is wrapped in `time.perf_counter`. So is `onnxruntime.InferenceSession.run`.
  Each call is recorded under the phase that is running when it happens. This
  separates the detection pass's inference from the check's, even though both
  use the same models. Generators such as `decode_range` are timed per item
  produced, so the time the consumer spends between items is not counted.
- **cProfile own time, tottime (trusted for leaf functions).** One profiler per
  phase, switched as the phases change. Because DirectML's `run` blocks until
  the GPU finishes, the own time of onnxruntime's Python `run` is the inference
  time. It equals the wall-clock inference timer to 0.1 s in every phase.
- **cProfile cumulative time, cumtime (not trusted).** On this Python (3.12.8),
  cProfile loses the call events of frames that call into onnxruntime.
  `detect_job`, `DetectorBank.detect_raw` and their callers are missing or show
  wrong cumtime, even with a single profiler. The wall-clock part tables below
  replace those numbers. The top-40 cumtime list is in the appendix only for
  completeness.
- **Overhead.** Profiled wall time was 496.6 s for the whole file, against 217.1 s
  at 4 workers in T01. The timer wrappers account for about 15 s of own time
  over 91k calls. cProfile adds more, mostly to code with many small Python
  calls (the tracker, `alpha_for`). The shares are reliable to a few points. The
  absolute seconds are a serial, instrumented run and are not the production
  wall time.

From `bench_out/baseline/quarantine/*_blurred.mp4.json`. Every file ran one
second-chance round and was quarantined.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 003939 | 2033 | 413.62 | 131.42 | 109.73 | 158.72 | 132.19 | 532.06 | 399.87 | 13.75 |
| 004100 | 984 | 217.11 | 70.10 | 38.81 | 101.18 | 70.32 | 280.41 | 210.09 | 7.02 |
| 004310 | 938 | 345.02 | 100.46 | 65.61 | 170.93 | 116.66 | 453.66 | 337.00 | 8.02 |
| 005035 | 3971 | 805.91 | 265.08 | 172.64 | 340.60 | 252.99 | 1031.31 | 778.32 | 27.59 |

**What double counts.** `second_chance_seconds` is a wrapper around time that
`encode_seconds` and `check_seconds` already include. In `batch.run_video`:

- `write_pass()` adds its time to `record.encode_seconds`
  (`record.encode_seconds = round(record.encode_seconds + time.time() - t1, 2)`).
  `check_pass()` adds its time to `record.check_seconds` in the same way. Both
  accumulate across calls.
- The second-chance block starts its clock `t3`, reruns the tracker, then calls
  `write_pass()` and `check_pass()` again, and sets
  `second_chance_seconds = time.time() - t3`.

So `second_chance_seconds` = second tracker run + second write + second check.
The second write is also in `encode_seconds` and the second check is also in
`check_seconds`. `encode_seconds` and `check_seconds` are the totals of both
rounds. `second_chance_seconds` is a subset of them, plus the tracker
(0.1–0.2 s).

The equation that holds is:

    wall ≈ detect + encode + check + (time no timer covers)

The time no timer covers is `probe`, `frame_times`, the first tracker run,
`zone.build`/`describe`, `keyframes`, `reorder_delay`, `unconfirmed_runs`,
building the jobs and pickling them to the pool, the gate's move, and the
sidecar write. That is the last column: 7–28 s, or 2–3 % of wall time.
Adding `second_chance_seconds` on top counts the second round twice. For
003939, 532 − 132 = 400, plus 14 s of untimed work, gives the 413.6 s wall.

**The second round costs as much as the first.** First round = (encode + check)
− second_chance:

| file | round 1 write + check s | round 2 write + check s (≈ second_chance) | round 2 share of wall |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 003939 | 136.3 | 132.2 | 32 % |
| 004100 | 69.7 | 70.3 | 32 % |
| 004310 | 119.9 | 116.7 | 34 % |
| 005035 | 260.3 | 253.0 | 31 % |

The profiled serial run shows the same split. Its record had encode 101.75,
check 244.08 and second_chance 172.61, so the first round was 173.2 s and the
second 172.6 s. The second round writes and checks the whole file again, but
on 004100 it was triggered by 11 seeds, of which 6 were kept as their own
masks.

## 4. What to fix first (ranked by expected wall saving at 4 workers)

The estimates use the T01 4-worker numbers and the phase shares measured
above. They are bounds and are not measured results.

1. **Make the second round incremental: re-encode and re-check only what it
   changes.** Today it repeats the whole write and the whole check, which is
   31–34 % of wall time on every file (table above). The frames whose mask
   changes are the seed frames ± `tail` and any tracks added. On 004100 that
   is 11 seeds. Segments with unchanged masks can keep their first-round
   encode, and their first-round check results still hold. Expected saving:
   most of `second_chance_seconds`, 50–65 s of 217 s on 004100 (about 25–30 %),
   and similar shares on the other files. This is the only item that reaches
   the 30 % gate on its own.
2. **Cut the YuNet blob preparation on the CPU.** `_pad32` (`copyMakeBorder`)
   plus `cv2.dnn.blobFromImage` on the 1920 and 1280 frames costs about 25 ms
   a frame. That is 48 s of the check's 243 s (20 %) and 24 s of detection's
   141 s (17 %) in the serial run. Options include a single resize+pad into a
   preallocated buffer, or feeding a cheaper layout. The check is the larger,
   CPU-heavy phase (61 % CPU), so this shortens the check at 4 workers by
   roughly 10–20 s on 004100, and less once item 1 halves the check.
3. **Batch the zone's palm windows.** `HandFinder.detect` is 44 % of the
   detection pass. It makes 24 separate `PalmDetector` inferences a frame
   (23616 calls at 2.3 ms each), plus 13 s of letterbox resizes. Detection is
   GPU-bound at 4 workers, and per-call DirectML dispatch is what those small
   calls pay for. One batched call per frame (or fewer, larger windows) should
   remove most of it. Expected saving: 10–25 s of the 70 s detect on 004100.
4. **Shrink the encode's per-frame full-frame mask work.** `redact` is 53 % of
   encode. The cost is in full-frame float alphas (`build_alpha` is called a
   second time per frame for `prevented`, `alpha_with_zone`/`cut`,
   `GaussianBlur` over the mask) and full-frame `.mean()` shares. Working on
   the face boxes' region of interest only would cut most of it. Expected
   saving: 10–15 s of the 39 s encode on 004100 (both rounds), half that if
   item 1 lands first.
5. **The check's own CPU extras.** These are `alpha_for` (zone masks rebuilt
   per frame, 21 s), `zone_changed` (13 s), `frame_noise` (7 s) and the
   two-stream decode (18.5 s). Together they are about 25 % of the check.
   The zone polygons are already computed in the detection pass and could be
   reused. Expected saving: 5–15 s on 004100.

Not worth touching: the tracker (0.2 s), the joins and copies (1.4 s), phase
correlation (3 s serial), and model loading (1–2 s a worker).
